## 1. Standard Wholesale Boutique Agreement Terms

A typical indie brand / boutique wholesale agreement covers the following:

### Minimum Order Quantity (MOQ)
- Independent designers commonly set MOQ between **6–24 units per style**
- Luxury/limited-edition brands often lower MOQ to 3–6 units to signal exclusivity
- Re-order minimums are typically lower than the initial opening order

### Pricing and Markup
- Standard wholesale markup: **2.0x–2.5x** the brand's wholesale price = retail price
- Wholesale price is typically **40–55% off retail**
- Example: $180 retail item → $72–90 wholesale cost to boutique

### Payment Terms
- Net 30 is the most common standard (boutique pays 30 days after invoice)
- Premium/new brands often require 50% deposit upfront + 50% on shipment
- Some boutiques push for consignment (pay after sold) — most indie brands avoid this

### Lead Time and Delivery
- Standard lead time: 4–8 weeks for handmade/limited-production goods
- Shipping costs: typically borne by the buyer (boutique)
- Delivery windows must be specified (especially for seasonal items)

### Return Policy
- Most wholesale agreements are **final sale** — returns not accepted unless item is defective
- Defective/damaged items: replacement or credit within 14–30 days of receipt
- Boutiques cannot return slow-selling stock

### Exclusivity Terms
- Regional or zip-code exclusivity is common for premium indie brands
- Typical radius protection: 5–15 miles in metro areas
- Time-limited exclusivity (6–12 months) is standard if offered at all
- Non-exclusive agreements are more common and easier to manage at scale

### Brand Usage Rights
- Boutique may use brand photography and product descriptions for their own sales channels
- Brand must approve any substantial editorial use or co-branding campaigns
- No modification of product images without approval

## 2. Standard Gift Broker Arrangement Terms

### How Gift Brokers Structure Deals
- Gift brokers act as intermediaries between brands and corporate/luxury gifting buyers (hotels, banks, event planners, high-net-worth clients)
- They typically work on **commission**, not retainer — 15–25% of the wholesale value of orders placed
- High-end boutique brokers may charge a **monthly retainer of $2,000–$5,000** plus reduced commission (8–15%)

### What Brokers Typically Deliver
- Introductions to their roster of corporate and luxury gift buyers
- Branded "gifting lookbook" or product sheet development
- Placement in curated gifting catalogs (physical or digital)
- Event-based gifting placements (fashion weeks, hotel amenity programs, VIP gift bags)

### Standard Commission Rates
| Arrangement | Rate |
|:---|:---|
| Commission-only broker | 15–25% of order value |
| Retainer + commission | $2,000–$5,000/mo + 8–15% |
| One-time placement fee | $500–$2,000 per gifting campaign |

### What Brands Should Watch For
- Exclusivity clauses that prevent working with other brokers in the same channel
- Long notice periods (60–90 days) to terminate agreements
- Ownership of client relationships — some brokers claim the buyer contacts belong to them

## 3. Celebrity Gifting Agreement Standard Practice

### Is There a Written Agreement?
- At the indie brand level, most celebrity gifting is **informal** — a product is sent with a note, no contract
- Higher-value or exclusivity-seeking brands increasingly use a **simple gifting letter or one-page agreement**
- Formal agreements become more common when the brand is paying a stylist fee or offering exclusivity

### What a Standard Gifting Agreement Typically Covers

**From the brand's perspective, the ideal agreement includes:**
- Product description and estimated retail value
- No obligation clause: recipient is not required to wear, post, or endorse the product
- If worn/used: request for @brand tag and photo credit in any published images
- Optional social post request: within 30–60 days if worn (typically negotiated)
- Photo rights: brand may repost any tagged content on its own channels
- Exclusivity window: brand may request a 30–60 day window before gifting the same item to competitors
- No payment: product is gifted at no cost; this is not a paid partnership unless separately agreed

### Industry Norms by Tier
| Recipient Tier | Typical Ask | Formal Agreement? |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Nano-influencer / emerging stylist | Tag if you wear it | Rarely |
| Mid-tier stylist (50K–500K following) | Tag + story post if worn | Sometimes |
| Celebrity stylist / A-list talent | Tag + feed post + photo rights | Often yes |
| Major celebrity (direct) | Full usage + paid partnership | Always |

### Photo Credit Standard
- Minimum standard: "@airplantnina" in caption or story tag if the product appears in any published photo
- Editorial use: brand is credited in the caption, not just tagged in a background tag

## 4. Best Outreach Platforms by Contact Type

### Luxury Boutique Buyers
| Platform | Notes |
|:---|:---|
| **Email (primary)** | Most effective for professional B2B outreach; buyers check email for vendor proposals |
| **LinkedIn** | Good for finding buyers and establishing credibility before emailing |
| **Trade shows** | NY NOW, Coterie, and regional gift shows are the main in-person channels |
| **Instagram DM** | Lower response rate for wholesale; more effective for brand awareness |

**Best approach:** Find the buyer on LinkedIn to get their direct email, then reach by email with a look book PDF attached.

### Gift Brokers
| Platform | Notes |
|:---|:---|
| **Email (primary)** | Brokers are business-oriented; email is their primary channel |
| **LinkedIn** | Many brokers maintain active LinkedIn profiles; good for warm intros |
| **Referral network** | Best gift broker leads come from other indie brand founders |

**Best approach:** LinkedIn connection request with a brief note → follow up with email and gifting catalog.

### Celebrity Stylists
| Platform | Notes |
|:---|:---|
| **Instagram DM (primary)** | Stylists are highly active on Instagram; DMs are routinely checked |
| **Email** | Effective when you can find the stylist's business email or agency contact |
| **LinkedIn** | Lower engagement for stylists vs. Instagram |
| **Agency cold call** | Some top stylists are repped by agencies (CAA, WME) — direct to agency is possible but harder |

**Best approach:** Follow + engage with their content first → DM with a concise gifting pitch + beautiful product image → follow up by email if they have one listed.
